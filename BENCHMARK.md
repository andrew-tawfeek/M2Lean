# Ideal membership: M2Lean vs Macaulean (benchmark)

Run 2026-09-29 on sentry (Slurm). **Final.** The benchmark was ended before
the largest dense-cofactor problems (degrees 8–12, planned for zenith)
completed; see "Not completed" below.

## Summary

- **The two proof methods cost about the same.** Once the time Lean spends
  just *stating* the goal is subtracted, M2Lean's proof (the kernel
  evaluates a checker; proof by reflection) and Macaulean's proof (rebuild
  the combination, then `simp` + `grind`) are close: 14.6 s vs 14.7 s at
  cofactor degree 4, 6.1 s vs 6.0 s at degree 3, and 21 s vs 33 s at
  32 generators.
- **The big difference is the statement.** Macaulean's goals are ordinary
  Lean terms over ℚ, and elaborating them dominates its time and grows
  roughly quadratically: 235 s of 268 s at 32 generators, 1136 s at 64,
  and over 30 minutes at cofactor degree 6. M2Lean's goals are sparse data
  literals and take a few seconds beyond its import cost.
- **Each tool has limits.** Macaulean's hard-coded
  `simp (maxSteps := 100000)` fails at 64 generators, and at cofactor
  degree 6 its goal statement alone exceeds 30 minutes. M2Lean completes
  every problem that finished here, but its memory grows steeply: 16 GB at
  64 generators, 15 GB at degree 6, and about 35 GB (killed by the
  out-of-memory killer) at degree 12.
- **Small problems favour Macaulean end to end**, because M2Lean pays 4.7 s
  and 6.7 GB to import Mathlib.

## What is compared

| Tool | How the goal is proved | Checked by Lean's kernel? |
|---|---|---|
| **M2Lean** `by macaulay2` | Macaulay2 supplies cofactors; `decide` makes the kernel evaluate `checkMembership f gs cs = true`; `checkMembership_sound` turns that into the theorem (proof by reflection) | Yes |
| **Macaulean** `m2idealmem` | Macaulay2 supplies cofactors; the tactic rebuilds `Σ cᵢ·gᵢ` as a Lean term and closes the goal with `simp` + `grind` | Yes |
| `m2lean-check` (baseline) | Compiled Lean checker run on the JSON certificate | **No** (trusts the compiler, JSON parser, and binary) |

Both tactics produce kernel-checked proofs; every completed proof depends
only on `propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound`. They prove different
statements:

- **M2Lean** proves ideal membership itself, `toMv n f ∈ spanOf n gs` in
  mathlib's `MvPolynomial (Fin n) ℚ`, with `f` and `gs` given as sparse
  data.
- **Macaulean** proves the value-level consequence: for `xᵢ : ℚ`, if every
  `gⱼ = 0` then `f = 0`, with everything written as ordinary Lean terms.

`m2lean-check` is listed only as a lower bound on the cost of the
arithmetic; it is not formal verification.

## Problems

All generated by Macaulay2 from the same polynomials for both tools
(`gen.m2`).

- **A (number of generators).** `k` variables, generators
  `gᵢ = xᵢ² − xᵢ₊₁` (indices cyclic), `f = Σ (xᵢ + xᵢ₊₁ + 1)²·gᵢ`.
  `f` has degree 4 and 3 to 576 terms.
- **B (cofactor size).** 4 variables, generators
  `x₁² − x₂, x₂² − x₃, x₃² − x₄, x₄² − x₁`,
  `f = Σ ((x₁ + x₂ + x₃ + x₄ + 1)ᵉ + xᵢ)·gᵢ`: dense cofactors of degree `e`.
- **C (`foo3`).** The 9-variable, 4-generator problem from Macaulean's own
  `MacauleanTest/Benchmarks.lean`.

## How to read the tables

Each problem is run three ways per tool:

- **Total:** the whole `lean` run (startup, imports, elaborating the goal,
  the tactic, the kernel check).
- **Statement:** the same file with the proof replaced by `sorry`
  (startup, imports, elaborating the goal only).
- **Proof = total − statement:** the cost of the tactic and kernel check.

Times are median wall-clock seconds over 3 repeats for runs under 60 s and
1 run otherwise. Proof times are differences of separate runs, so treat
small values (under about 1 s) as noise. "Kernel" is the profiler's
`type checking` time, part of the proof time. Import cost is included in
both statement and total: 4.7 s for M2Lean (imports Mathlib) and 1.0 s for
Macaulean. All numbers below are from sentry.

## Where Macaulean spends its time

Most of Macaulean's time goes to Lean elaborating the goal statement, not to
Macaulean's proof. At 32 generators the statement alone takes 235.5 of the
268.1 s; at 64 it takes 1136 s. The profiler attributes it to typeclass
inference (about 197 s at 32 generators, 903 s at 64), which is the same
whether or not the tactic runs: every `+`, `*`, `^` and numeral in the long
polynomial over ℚ needs instances resolved. Statement time grows about 4×
per doubling of the goal (13.7 → 55.6 → 235.5 → 1136 s for 72 → 144 →
288 → 576 terms), i.e. roughly quadratically in the number of terms. This
is Lean's term elaboration, not Macaulean's code, but Macaulean users pay
it because they write goals as terms.

A same-machine check on zenith agrees: 26.8 s of 29.8 s at k = 16 and
77.1 s of 85.2 s at k = 32 are the statement alone.

## What this means

- **Proof by reflection is not what makes M2Lean faster here.** On proof
  cost alone the methods are close. M2Lean's proof is about 1.5× cheaper
  at 32 generators; at cofactor degrees 2–4 they are equal.
- **The input format is.** M2Lean's sparse data literals elaborate
  cheaply; Macaulean's term goals do not. That is a usability trade-off:
  Macaulean accepts the goals users naturally write, while M2Lean needs
  the goal as data (a translation step from terms would count against it).
- **Limits differ.** Macaulean hits its fixed `simp` step budget at
  64 generators, and its goal statements become impractical at a few
  hundred terms. M2Lean handles those sizes, but its kernel check's memory
  grows steeply (15–16 GB at the largest completed problems, about 35 GB
  at cofactor degree 12).
- **M2Lean's fixed cost is higher**: 4.7 s and 6.7 GB just to import
  Mathlib, which is why Macaulean wins end to end on the smallest problems.
- **`m2lean-check` stays under 0.2 s** on every problem: checking a
  certificate is cheap, and nearly all of either tactic's cost is the cost
  of doing it inside Lean.

## Not completed

Cofactor degrees 8, 10 and 12 were planned for zenith (Ryzen 7 9800X3D,
48 GB WSL 2 VM) and did not finish:

- The first attempt was killed when one `lean` process (almost certainly
  M2Lean at degree 12) reached about 35 GB and triggered the kernel's
  out-of-memory killer, which also took down the other runs.
- Two reruns (with per-run memory caps on the second) stopped without
  producing output; the cause was not determined, and the benchmark was
  ended there.

Given the trends above, Macaulean would not have finished these within the
30-minute limit (its statement alone already exceeds it at degree 6).

## Setup

- **sentry:** Xeon E5-1620 v2 (4 cores / 8 threads, 3.7 GHz), 30 GB,
  Debian 12. Slurm job arrays, 2 tasks at a time, 4 CPUs each (briefly 3
  at a time while two arrays overlapped).
- Macaulay2 1.26.06; M2Lean `core` branch (Lean 4.32.0, pinned Mathlib);
  Macaulean commit `6cdedde` (Lean 4.33.1).
- 30-minute limit per run. Goal statements never start with a unary minus,
  to avoid a Macaulean bug (see below).

## Macaulean issues found

Logged with repros in `/shared/m2lean-bench/macaulean-bugs.md` for PRs:

1. A hypothesis starting with a unary minus (`h : -x + y = 0`) makes
   `m2idealmem` fail with a kernel error ("declaration has free variables");
   `y - x = 0` works. `toPolynomialExpr?` has no `Neg.neg` case.
2. `simp (maxSteps := 100000)` is hard-coded, which caps problem size
   (A, k = 64).
